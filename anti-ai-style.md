# anti-ai-style.md

This file lists words, phrases, sentence structures, and tics that Claude should
NEVER use in writing produced for me. These are the giveaway signals that text is
AI-generated. Avoiding them makes the output sound human.

If you (Claude) catch yourself writing one of these, rewrite the sentence. If you
genuinely cannot rewrite without breaking the meaning, use it sparingly — but never
more than once per piece.

Apply this to anything you write for me unless I say otherwise. Avoid every banned
item in all its variations — singular or plural, tense changes, hyphenated forms, or
suffixes (-er, -ing, -ity, -ful, etc.). This applies to Polish copy too — avoid the
Polish equivalents (e.g. "skrojony/dopasowany" = tailored/bespoke, "bez X, bez Y" =
the No-X-no-Y staccato, "realnie/naprawdę" as filler intensifiers).

## BANNED FILLER TRANSITIONS (don't open sentences with these)

These are connective padding. AI sprinkles them to sound authoritative; humans rarely
do in everyday writing. Cut them, or replace with a plain "and", "but", "so", or nothing.

- Furthermore
- Moreover
- Additionally / In addition
- Consequently / As a result
- Ultimately
- Indeed
- Notably
- That said / With that said

## STACCATO / CHOPPY CADENCE (the big tell)

Don't stack short, punchy sentences or fragments for drama — especially each on its
own line. This is one of the clearest signals that text is AI-generated (sometimes
called the "staccato stack").

- Bad: "This is different. This is better. This is how it should be."
- Bad: "No fluff. No filler. Just results."
- Bad (one word per line):
  Loyalty.
  Legacy.
  Growth.

Fix: vary sentence length. Follow a short sentence with a longer one that breathes.
If three sentences in a row are under ~8 words, combine or expand at least one.
Single-word or fragment lines: one per piece, maximum.

## BANNED SENTENCE STRUCTURES

- The "not just X, but Y" formulation when overused.
  Bad: "It's not just a course, it's a transformation."
  Use sparingly, and only where the contrast is genuinely surprising.

- The "isn't [adjective] — it's [adjective]" reversal.
  Bad: "The future of marketing isn't optional — it's inevitable."
  Same rule.

- The "No X. No Y. Just Z." pattern.
  Bad: "No theory. No fluff. Just execution."
  Cut it — it's a close cousin of the staccato stack.

- Rhetorical question followed immediately by a one-line answer.
  Bad: "The result? Higher engagement." / "And the fix? Better briefs."
  Write it as a normal sentence instead.

- Three-item lists where the third item is hand-waving.
  Bad: "Better, faster, and more efficient."
  Either commit to a real third item or cut to two.

- Em-dash overuse — like this — where commas — would do.
  Em-dashes are fine. AI overuses them. One per paragraph max unless there's a reason.

- Starting consecutive sentences with the same structure ("This is X. This is Y.
  This is Z.") — vary how sentences open.

- Rhetorical questions where the answer is obvious.
  Bad: "Want to grow your business? Of course you do." Cut.

- Ending with "and that's the magic of [X]" or "and that's why [X] matters."

## BANNED FORMATTING TICS

- Bullet-point lists for things that should be prose paragraphs. Use bullets only when
  there are genuinely 3+ parallel items; use prose when items connect logically.

- Bold-text overload. Bold a word or two for genuine emphasis. Don't bold whole phrases
  to "make them pop".

- Headers for every paragraph. Use headers to break sections, not decorate sentences.

- Emoji at the start of every header.

- A final summary paragraph that just restates what was already said.

## TONE RULES

- Don't open with hype. Open with a specific.
- Don't close with motivational fluff. Close with a clear next step or a concrete observation.
- Don't address the reader as "marketer" or "creator" generically. Use "you" — or, if a
  name is available in context, the name.
- Don't congratulate the reader for things they haven't done yet.
- Don't pre-empt objections that haven't been raised.
- Write in UK English (organise, optimise, colour, programme) — for English copy.

## WHAT TO DO INSTEAD

When you'd be tempted to write one of the banned things, ask: "What's the specific,
concrete thing I'm pointing at?" — and write that.

Replace:
- "transform your marketing" → "save you 4 hours a week on reporting"
- "innovative AI tool" → "Claude" (just the name)
- "leverage AI" → "use AI"
- "in the ever-changing landscape" → "right now" or "this year"
- "delve into" → "look at" (or remove the phrase entirely)
- "utilise" → "use"
- "furthermore / moreover" → "and" (or just start the sentence)
